# Beyond the Sun — Rules summary

**Rules target:** the English base game's second printing. **Coverage:** implementation-oriented rules summary; card-by-card manifests are a required implementation deliverable.

The authority is Rio Grande Games' [combined rulebook and setup sheets](https://www.riograndegames.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/08/Beyond-the-Sun-2nd-Print-Rules-Combined.pdf), including its Rule and Card Clarifications section, followed by the publisher's [errata](https://www.riograndegames.com/games/beyond-the-sun/). References below use printed rulebook page numbers; the combined PDF places setup sheets before the numbered rulebook. This document records mechanics, not the rulebook's narrative or illustrations.

## Objective and components

Two to four factions compete for victory points through technology, automation, colonies, outposts, achievements, and event effects. There is no fixed turn count.

Each faction has one Action pawn, twenty resource cubes, eight Food production discs, eight Ore production discs, and achievement/placeholder markers. Cubes have Supply, Population, and ship-level 1–4 states; they are not rolled. Research commits a cube permanently. Ships can return to Supply through colonization, keeping the resource cycle moving. Ore and placeholder markers have no effective component limit.

The base content inventory is 46 Technology cards, 20 Events, 24 Systems split evenly between decks A and B, ten Achievements, four guild covers, Basic and Advanced faction mats, player-count action overlays, and two exploration-board faces. Exact definitions belong in reviewed manifests before use.

## Setup

The shared tree begins with all four Level I technologies face up in randomized positions. Higher levels are discovered during play. Prepare six Level II events and six Level III events: each group includes its two fixed light-bordered events and four randomly selected others. These cover the matching undiscovered slots. Guild action spaces begin covered.

For four players, reveal two randomly chosen Starting Systems from deck A, shuffle the remaining Starting Systems into that deck, and reveal two deck B systems. Achievements always include Transcendence and Empire plus one randomly selected A achievement and one B achievement. The introductory preset uses Warlord and Technologist for those two selections.

Choose the starting player and preserve that marker throughout the game. Basic setup fills Supply columns, covers all eight spaces of each production track, provides two available Population and one level-1 ship at Sol, then applies the faction's printed setup. First and second players receive one Ore; third and fourth receive two. Faction-specific starting changes must come from the selected mat.

| Players | Required changes from four-player setup |
| --- | --- |
| 4 | Four-player Basic Spacefaring spaces; standard exploration face; four achievement claims trigger endgame. |
| 3 | Three-player Basic Spacefaring overlay; second slot of the selected A achievement unavailable; three claims trigger endgame. |
| 2 | Two-player overlay and exploration face; only one Starting System from A revealed; Disciples of the Cosmic Tabernacle unavailable; second A-achievement slot unavailable; both players start with one Ore; three claims trigger endgame. |

For Advanced play, everyone uses Advanced mats. After the shared setup, choose factions in reverse player order. Each mat has its own Supply/production configuration, setup, and innovation conditions. Some innovations persist only while a production threshold is satisfied. Basic and Advanced mats must not be mixed. See the publisher's setup sheets.

## Turn structure

Turns proceed clockwise: **Action → Production → Achievement**. All required effects and choices finish before moving on. The digital game keeps turns sequential; it does not use the rulebook's optional agreement to overlap another player's action with production.

### Action

Move the Action pawn to a legal open space and execute its action. Usually it must leave its current space, cannot share a space, and can use a technology's spaces only after that faction has researched it. Board-printed actions need no research. Different spaces on the same card can repeat the same action. The last Basic Spacefaring space has a repeat exception; player-count overlays and additional slot costs must be respected.

Pay costs before effects. A cost that returns Population and an effect that uses Population require separate cubes. Do not accept an action whose complete result deliberately changes nothing. Optional portions may be omitted when the overall action still changes state. Card-specific exceptions can permit copying occupied actions. See pp. 6–7, 12–14, 23.

The four-player Basic Spacefaring actions provide Level I research, Level II research for two Ore, optional construction of a level-1 ship followed by up to two jumps, and conversion of Supply to Population plus one Ore. Exact spaces, surcharges, repeatability, and overlays must be transcribed rather than inferred from these descriptions.

### Production

Choose exactly one branch:

| Branch | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Population growth | For each exposed Food-production letter, turn one Supply cube from that specific column into available Population. An empty column produces nothing; another column cannot substitute. |
| Ore production | Gain Ore equal to the exposed Ore-production symbols. |
| Resource trade | Make any number of legal exchanges in any combination, using the rates below. No ordinary production is added. |

Trade rates are three Ore for one Supply cube converted to Population; one Ore to decommission one ship anywhere into available Population; or one available Population returned to Supply for one Ore. Recheck control when decommissioning changes a fleet.

Production takes the full possible yield. Direct Supply effects and trades take cubes from the leftmost nonempty column unless overridden. Returned cubes fill empty Supply spaces from right to left; overflow extends column A. Food is a production track, not a spendable resource. See pp. 8–9, 13–14.

### Achievement

Claim at most one eligible achievement on the active faction's turn. Claiming is mandatory if an eligible open slot exists; when several qualify, choose one. A faction cannot claim the same achievement twice. Fill slots left to right; a claim remains even if its condition is later lost. Placeholder discs on other components are not achievement claims. See p. 10.

## Research and discovery

Research requires an effect permitting the target level, the required Population, and that slot's prerequisites. Most slots require all directly connected predecessor technologies; some Level IIII slots have a printed two-of-three requirement. An eye-marked research effect permits only an already discovered technology.

Place the research cube permanently beside the slot. Another faction's research cube does not block yours. For an undiscovered Level II or III slot, reveal and resolve its Event before selecting the technology. Retain ongoing events as instructed; fixed events can uncover guild actions.

For discovery, determine the permissible type from predecessor cards' outgoing triangles; choose a type when different predecessor types allow a choice. Reveal from the appropriate deck until two cards match their incoming triangle, including hybrids. Choose one for the shared slot. Put the rejected and nonmatching cards on the deck bottom in randomized order; do not shuffle the remaining deck.

Receive the researched card's immediate bonus, including when another faction discovered it earlier. Action boxes are future actions, not immediate bonuses. Discovery grants no automatic research to other factions. Publicly revealed candidate cards are not a private hand. See pp. 14–16.

Private Technologies from particular System bonuses are personal tableau cards: they require no research cube, grant their immediate bonus, allow only their owner's actions, score for their level, and cannot be researched or copied by others. Their name does not make them hidden cards. Their exact acquisition procedure must follow the System effect. See p. 23.

## Automation

An automation effect moves the leftmost disc of the indicated production track onto the next Automation position. It improves production by exposing track symbols. Automation is permanent; outposts can be lost. Use a placeholder when no matching production disc remains. Score the track's printed cumulative points; each level beyond seven adds one point. A later returned production disc does not replace an existing placeholder. See p. 17 and clarifications.

## Fleets, jumps, and control

Build a ship of the specified level at Sol or a controlled shipyard using the effect's required cube. Upgrades change owned ships' levels, capped at four. Fleet power is the sum of ship levels at a location, not its ship count.

One jump point moves one ship across one connected edge. Split the allowance among ships or spend several points on one; unused points expire. Resolve the whole jump plan simultaneously, then check control and bonuses. A separate upgrade effect can change control before a subsequent jump effect.

Sol and Deep Space cannot be controlled. Systems and shipyards can. An incoming faction must exceed an incumbent's power to take control; a tie does not displace the incumbent. When the incumbent withdraws, a uniquely strongest remaining rival takes control; a tie among remaining rivals can leave the system uncontrolled. An incumbent leaving an otherwise empty system retains its outpost. The complete tie decision table needs explicit fixtures before implementation; do not reduce control to a simple maximum-power lookup.

Gaining control places the leftmost matching production disc as an outpost and resolves any gain-control bonus. Multi-specialty systems permit a disc-type choice. Losing control returns the disc to its track's rightmost empty position. Placeholders cover exhausted disc supplies. Resolve control whenever relevant board state changes, including on another faction's turn. Multiple simultaneous gain-control bonuses may require their owner's ordering choice. Control does not destroy opposing ships. See pp. 17–19.

## Colonization

An ordinary colonization effect needs control of a colonizable System card and sufficient local fleet power. Sol, Deep Space, and shipyards cannot be colonized. Resolve in this order:

1. Select owned ships at the System with combined power at least its requirement. Overpayment gives no change.
2. Return those cubes to Supply, filling from right to left.
3. Move all remaining ships there, including opponents' ships, to Deep Space.
4. Take the System and its outpost into the faction's colony area; refill from the matching deck, falling back to the other deck if exhausted. Leave the slot empty if both are exhausted.
5. Add a second matching production disc, choosing a permitted specialty if applicable; use a placeholder when necessary.
6. Resolve the colonization bonus. The replacement System is already present for any resulting jumps.

A colony is permanent territory and no longer a controlled exploration location. Do not repeat its gain-control bonus when colonizing. See pp. 20–22.

## Endgame and scoring

The endgame threshold counts total claims across factions and cards: **three for two or three players, four for four players**. Finish the current round, then play one additional full round. More achievements may be claimed during those remaining turns. Everyone finishes with equal turns. See pp. 10–11 and the publisher's player-count clarification.

Score researched Levels I/II/III for 1/2/3 points each, personal technologies for their level, Level IIII effects as printed, cumulative automation, colony values, one point per uncolonized outpost, applicable Events, and achievements. At Sol and Deep Space, each faction tied for greatest military power receives that location's one point under the scoring rule. Do not count a colony's discs again as outposts.

Break score ties by fewest Food plus Ore discs remaining on the faction mat, then most available Population, then most Ore. A remaining tie is a shared victory. Display each comparison rather than inventing a seat-order winner.

## Content verification before shipping

This summary does not specify every card's exact cost, bonus, achievement threshold, or faction innovation. Before those effects enter play, record stable IDs, copy counts, exact mechanics, source location, effect ordering, targets, optional choices, scoring, and reviewed clarifications. Record both board geometries, all action slots/overlays, prerequisite edges, mat columns/tracks, and card backs.

Review all 46 technologies, 20 events, 24 systems, ten achievements, Basic and Advanced mats, and guild effects. Coverage must include private technologies, copied actions, persistent scoring events, removed-from-game ships, attached systems, chained bonuses, depleted decks, exhausted disc tracks, and overflow Supply. Examples requiring individual treatment include Quantum Data Exchange, Museum of Retired Spacecraft, Dyson Sphere Mk. I, Specialization, and the Advanced United Nations trade innovation.

Any unresolved interaction gets a source-linked issue and blocks that affected content from release. It must not silently become a house rule. The Expert Technology Tableau and expansion rules require separate review before being added.
